Human rights assessment
HRA-001 · version 1.0 · current · verified 12 October 2026 · owner: Chad Johnston

This assessment covers the 4ormIQ consumer trust layer and the evidence store behind it, against the Canadian Human Rights Act and the Alberta and British Columbia human rights codes.

1. Scope

The assessment covers automated retrieval, the ranking of favourable and unfavourable evidence, and the consumer-facing result page. It does not cover the firm-side surface, which is assessed separately under SEC-001.

2. Protected grounds

No protected ground is collected, inferred or used as an input to any ranking. Where a record names one, it is sealed until selected and never carried into a comparison.

3. Findings

Two findings, both closed. The retrieval window favoured recency in a way that advantaged larger firms with more coverage; corrected in the time-window rule. The second concerned language in the result page, corrected in the conversation standard.

The three changes.
1. A week with no items in it is not a quiet week, it is a week with no data. Leading empty weeks come out of the baseline entirely instead of counting as zero.
2. The baseline is the median of the real weeks, not the mean, so one quiet week cannot drag the whole scale.
3. Under four real weeks, the gauge shows – and "building a baseline, 3 of 4 weeks" instead of a number. It already has that state in the design, it is just never switched on.

After that, 50 stays a normal week, the arc only reaches 100 when a topic genuinely runs at double its own median, and the delta is in points against the same week last week. Right now, honestly, two of the four do not have enough history to score and should say so.
